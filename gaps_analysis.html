Read this before you present

Three problems are about where the list came from, not about the literature. They will get caught in a viva faster than any missing citation.

  • Gaps 1, 2 and 3 are the paper's own Limitations section. Section 8 says verbatim that the attack "is not evaluated against hybrid pipelines and reranking mechanisms" and that it "does not ensure transferability when the reference and target embedding models have different model architectures — we acknowledge these gaps." Section 5.2 says "we focus on the email scenario … and leave the other settings as future directions." Presenting these as gaps you identified is a claim the paper contradicts on its own page 13. Attribute them.
  • Gaps 5 and 6 are contradicted by the paper you are presenting. Appendix C runs the attack on MS COCO image-to-text retrieval with OpenCLIP ViT-B-32 and reports near-perfect recall. Section 4.2 and Figure 6 test triggers moved across positions, Figure 7 scatters trigger tokens through the document, and Section 6 states "our attack is position-agnostic (recall Figure 6), position-based defenses are excluded by design." Both slides claim gaps the authors already filled.
  • Gap 8 contradicts your own Gap 3. Slide 21 says the evaluation focuses too narrowly on email; slide 18 then lists email security as an unexplored gap. Email is the one setting the paper covers in depth — Enron corpus, AutoGen single-agent, Magentic-One multi-agent, four attack objectives. Pick one position.

01

Hybrid retrieval and reranking robustness

Slides 18, 19 · verdict: narrowed
Narrowed
As stated

"Does the attack work against hybrid retrieval? Can BM25 reduce attack success? Can cross-encoder rerankers detect malicious candidates?"

Already answered
  • 2601.07072 §8The paper names this limitation itself and footnotes Weaviate's hybrid search docs. Not your finding.
  • 2603.18034
    Mar 2026    Semantic Chameleon answers all three questions on a 67,941-document Security Stack Exchange corpus: hybrid BM25 + vector retrieval cuts gradient-guided poisoning from 38% to 0% across 50 attacks; attackers who jointly optimize sparse and dense signals recover only 20–44%.
  • 2606.11265
    Jun 2026    CRCP shows existing poisoning "substantially degrades after reranking despite achieving high retrieval-stage relevance," blames retrieval granularity mismatch under chunking, and builds a chunk-aware, rerank-consistent attack.
  • 2603.22934
    Mar 2026    ProGRank is a training-free retriever-side reranking defense evaluated end-to-end and under adaptive evasive attacks.
  • 2602.06616
    Feb 2026    Confundo fine-tunes an LLM as a poison generator specifically because "content is often processed before use, which can fragment the poison."
  • 2605.01591 · 2601.20283 · 2512.23307The adversarial-IR line has attacked cross-encoders and LLM listwise rerankers (CRAFT; single-word attacks at up to 91% success on BERT/monoT5) and built certified defenses (RobustMask).
What survives

A narrow but real sliver. Semantic Chameleon uses white-box GCG at the retrieval stage only; CRCP targets knowledge poisoning, not instruction injection through an agent. Nobody has run a black-box, query-natural, hybrid- and rerank-aware trigger optimization end-to-end through a tool-using agent. That is the claim you can defend — not "hybrid retrieval is unstudied."

02

Cross-model trigger transferability

Slides 18, 20 · verdict: narrowed, premise partly dissolved
Narrowed
As stated

"A realistic attacker may not know the target embedding model." Proposed direction: a model-agnostic transferable trigger — one trigger, many models.

Already answered
  • 2601.07072 §4.2, §8Figure 5 already measures cross-model transfer. OpenAI-built triggers average 74% recall and break 7 of 8 targets above 60%; Qwen-family triggers transfer within the family; Q3-0.6B → OpenAI drops to 10%. The Limitations section flags the architecture-mismatch case.
  • 2602.00364
    30 Jan 2026    "Someone Hid It!" — posted nineteen days after the paper you are presenting — generates transferable injection tokens from zero-shot surrogate LLMs with no victim queries and no victim model knowledge. That is your slide-20 research direction, already published.
  • 2607.01276
    Jul 2026    Embedding Inference Attack undercuts the premise: observing only the unordered retrieved set, an adversary can identify which embedding model is deployed from a candidate list — and some queries stay discriminative even behind a reranker. If you can fingerprint the model, you do not need a universal trigger.
  • 2310.19156 · 2412.20953Zhong et al. (EMNLP 2023) established within-family transfer and out-of-domain generalization; GASLITE (ACM CCS 2025) benchmarked nine models and stated it did not optimize for transferability.
  • 2511.19257
    Nov 2025    Medusa demonstrates the standard recipe — surrogate model ensemble plus invariant risk minimization — for black-box cross-modal transfer.
What survives

Cross-architecture transfer for black-box CEM triggers specifically is still weak, and no one has chained fingerprinting to targeted trigger construction. Reframe from "build a universal trigger" to "fingerprint, then target" — that is novel and it is the operationally realistic path.

03

Limited agentic environments

Slides 18, 21 · verdict: narrowed
Narrowed
As stated

Coding assistants, software engineering agents, enterprise agents, cloud infrastructure agents, log analysis agents and database agents "remain insufficiently explored."

Already answered
  • 2605.17986
    May 2026    LivePI covers seven input surfaces — email, chat, web, local files, repositories, wallet — on a real VM, including unsafe code retrieval and execution, across GPT-5.3-Codex, Claude Opus 4.6, Gemini 3.1 Pro, Kimi K2.5 and GLM-5. Attack success 10.7–29.6%.
  • 2605.24659
    May 2026    IterInject runs extension experiments on Claude Code, a production coding agent.
  • 2607.10490
    Jul 2026    NetInjectBench — 130 scenarios over tickets, alerts, logs, runbooks and ChatOps. Naive execution hits an 82.5% unsafe tool-action rate; a metadata-aware policy gate reaches 0/240.
  • 2605.24245
    May 2026    Deep-research agents (STORM, Co-STORM, OmniThink) poisoned via user-generated content — and this one does model retrieval competition, exploiting repeated retrieval of the same Reddit/Wikipedia pages across a query cluster. Closest existing work to a non-email retrieval-barrier attack.
  • CSA, 2026Field evidence: GrafanaGhost poisoned log entries so Grafana's AI assistant exfiltrated data via markdown image rendering — a log-analysis agent compromised in the wild. Google reports a 32% relative rise in malicious IPI content between Nov 2025 and Feb 2026.
  • 2604.03870 · 2605.17830Six defenses × four IPI vectors × nine backbones in multi-step tool environments; and longitudinal memory contamination in memory-equipped agents.
What survives

The environments are covered — but almost all of that work hands the payload to the agent. None of it makes the malicious document win a retrieval competition against a realistic code, log or schema corpus. Re-scope to "the retrieval barrier has only been crossed in email and web corpora" and the gap holds.

04

Weak retrieval-level defenses

Slides 18, 22 — including "Most Important Gap" · verdict: answered as written
Answered
As stated

"There is still no strong and general-purpose retrieval-aware defense against indirect prompt injection." Slide 18 flags this as the most important gap of the nine.

Already answered
  • 2601.07072 §6The paper tests only three lightweight defenses and explicitly rules fine-tuning defenses (SecAlign, DataSentinel, StruQ) out of scope. "We tested three and they failed" is not evidence that no strong defense exists.
  • 2405.15556RobustRAG gives certifiable robustness — formal lower bounds on response quality against an adaptive attacker with full knowledge of the defense. This alone falsifies the slide as worded.
  • 2509.23519
    Sep 2025    ReliabilityRAG — provably robust, maximum-independent-set filtering over a document graph, explicitly built for RAG-based web search.
  • 2608.13010
    Aug 2026    RAGSieve reaches 95.2% AUROC and drops attack success from 67.4% to 14.0%, beating the GMTP and CleanBase baselines. Note the baselines — this is a crowded field, not an empty one.
  • 2512.24268 · 2602.04711 · 2510.09710RAGPart & RAGMask (retrieval-stage, four retrievers, four attacks), SDAG (block-sparse document attention), SeCon-RAG (two-stage semantic filtering).
  • 2605.01782 · 2606.25721Forensics has arrived too: character-level poison traceback (Needle-in-RAG) and token-influence attribution (TRACE).
What survives — and this is the good version

The April 2026 survey that cites your paper gives you a far stronger framing, with two named structural mismatches: a signal mismatch (attacks now produce fluent, retrievable payloads while defenses still hunt poison as a semantic anomaly) and a stage mismatch (defensive effort concentrates downstream while the persistent harm is created upstream at the corpus layer, which "remains underdeveloped"). Its sharpest line: "few defenses are evaluated against attackers aware of the defense," making reported robustness "systematically optimistic." Rewrite slide 22 around adaptive evaluation, not absence.

06

Positional dependency of triggers

Slide 18 · verdict: answered, contradicted by the paper
Answered
Already answered
  • 2601.07072 §4.2
    Figure 6    "Across positions" is a named subsection. A prefix optimized at position 0 keeps over 50% Recall@5 when moved elsewhere for most models. OpenAI is the exception — 80% collapses to nearly 0% at position 20 — but re-optimizing at the end recovers 60%.
  • 2601.07072
    Figure 7    Token dispersion goes further: scatter the trigger tokens randomly through the document and GTE and Q3-4B stay above 90% Recall@5. OpenAI falls to 6%.
  • 2601.07072 §6And the conclusion is stated outright: "because our attack is position-agnostic (recall Figure 6), position-based defenses are excluded by design."
  • 2312.14197
    KDD 2025    BIPIA already injects malicious instructions at the beginning, middle and end of external content to measure positional bias.
What survives — a small, genuinely interesting one

Nobody explains why OpenAI's embeddings are the outlier. The paper only conjectures that they "encode positional information more explicitly." Whether deliberately position-sensitive embeddings are a viable architectural defense is unstudied — and that is a defense contribution, not a gap in attack coverage. It is worth one slide, reframed.

Query-specific trigger optimization

Slide 18 · verdict: survives, but only one half of it
Survives
The premise is correct

The attack is per-query: "on each target query, we generate a single malicious item and inject it into the corpus," at roughly $0.21 per target query on OpenAI embeddings.

Already answered — the attack half
  • 2601.07072
    Figure 8    The paper already shows one trigger generalizes across different attack fragments, lifting similarity from about −0.1 to 0.76. Only the query axis stays fixed.
  • 2310.19156
    EMNLP 2023    Query-agnostic corpus poisoning is three years old: 50 passages optimized on Natural Questions mislead over 94% of questions in financial documents and online forums.
  • 2412.20953
    CCS 2025    GASLITE targets concept-level query distributions and states that single-query SEO is "completely solved."
  • 2602.00364 · 2602.06616 · 2606.01212Query-agnostic black-box attacks; Confundo handles users not issuing the anticipated query; DiscourseFlip manipulates opinion across a multi-topic query network.
What survives — the defense half

The survey's taxonomy splits targets into T1 (a single known query) and T2 (claim manipulation across a query distribution). T1 dominates the literature. For T2 it finds attacks are "few," defenses are "nearly absent," and no benchmark evaluates it at all — it calls this a structural blind spot. So the gap is real, but it is not "build a query-agnostic attack." It is build the T2 benchmark and the first T2 defense.

Three contributions that would actually survive review

Built only from what is left standing above. Ranked by how defensible the novelty claim is today.

Strongest claim

The retrieval barrier under a production pipeline

Take the paper's black-box CEM trigger and push it through what real systems deploy: BM25 + dense hybrid fusion, document chunking, and a cross-encoder or LLM reranker — then measure end-to-end through a tool-using agent, not at Recall@5.

Why it survives: Semantic Chameleon answered the hybrid question with white-box GCG at the retrieval stage only. CRCP answered the chunk-and-rerank question for knowledge poisoning, not for instruction injection through an agent. The intersection — black-box, natural queries, full pipeline, agent outcome — is unoccupied. You must cite and differentiate from both, or a reviewer will assume you missed them.

Most novel

Fingerprint, then target

Chain embedding-model identification to trigger construction. Use the Embedding Inference Attack to determine which embedding model a deployment runs from black-box retrieval output alone, then optimize a CEM trigger against exactly that model — sidestepping transferability rather than solving it.

Why it survives: the fingerprinting paper stops at identification. The retrieval-barrier paper stops at "the adversary likely needs a good guess of the target's embedding architecture." Nobody has joined them, and the join turns a stated limitation into a working attack. It also gives you a clean defense contribution: does query-side noise or reranking break the fingerprint?

Best defense-side paper

Provenance-weighted retrieval, adaptively evaluated

Build a source-authority signal into the retrieval score, then evaluate it against an attacker who knows the defense exists and optimizes against it — including at the T2 query-distribution level, where no benchmark exists yet.

Why it survives: it sits on the two things the field admits are missing — an underdeveloped upstream defense layer, and robustness numbers that are "systematically optimistic" because attackers were not defense-aware. Building the T2 evaluation harness is itself a contribution, since none exists.
